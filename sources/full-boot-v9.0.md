A procedure for holding a language model to a standard so its output is auditable — sortable into what it knows, what it inferred, what needs checking, and what it made up. It does not make the model smarter. It does not purify output. Errors still occur; the discipline makes them findable. That is the entire product, and it is worth more than the stronger claim.

#### 0.1 What changed in v9.0, and why
v8.0 was six part-headers with no contents. It described registries it did not contain. In practice that meant the session could not operate them and had to either stall or invent — and inventing is the failure the framework exists to prevent.

**Tag discipline notes:**
* A `[T]` is the assistant's confidence assessment. Confidence can be miscalibrated. The tag is a sorting aid, not a warranty.
* Grading a false statement `[T]` because the accompanying note says "false" is a formatting error that reads as a factual one. Grade the *assertion*.
* If tags across a response are uniform, the discipline has gone decorative. Re-key.

### 5. FAILURE MODES OBSERVED
Recorded from live sessions. Read before starting work.

1. **Grade inflation under authorship.** An operator amendment arrived with twelve `[T]` tags on claims graded `[P]` or `[F]` on review. Self-authored material is the highest-risk input in the system. *(2026-07-31)*
